## General Overview

<DevastationEvoker /> overall is a mid-to-high-tier spec this season. It is generally good at most of what it can do but also has some big shortcomings which it cannot compensate. Additionally it also has some issues which are as old as the spec and overall there is some pretty heavy resentment towards the spec because of that.

## Devastation in Raid 

Overall <DevastationEvoker/> is pretty good in the current raid. Its lacking a bit in Single Target but makes up for that with some massive cleave.

<Scalecommander /> is the pick for nearly all fights because of the wide-reaching spread cleave requirements on all fights. The exception is <WowheadLink kind="npc" id={257347}>**Sszorak**</WowheadLink>, which still favors <Scalecommander /> for players that have unlearned the basics of being a caster, but otherwise <Flameshaper /> is a solid pick there.

However this raid exposes what I prophesized in Season 1:

<RisingFury /> is pretty bad because of a few reasons:

- On **Entombed Sentinels** you start gaining value... into the first intermission.
- On **The Lost Explorers**,**Vashnik** and **Twin Fangs** its pretty bad because <Scalecommander /> generally prefers other talents that offer more cleave with better Essence economy
- On **The Coiled Altar** and **Ula'tek** it's bad because of aforementioned reasons and additionally the amp windows being weirdly placed so that you cannot really take advantage. 
    - Technically a build with 4 points in <RisingFury /> should be the best on **Ula'Tek** but it seems to feel pretty bad to play.
    - It's no surprise that as a result, <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> was preferred for **Ula'tek** due to being more frontloaded/bursty, despite it losing ~half of its <Duplicate /> uptime for the same reasons.

All this leads to most players resenting <RisingFury /> or rather how it works right now. The most common statements are:

- The ramp is too long
- Its not really that strong
- Unbound Flame isn't worth the QoL loss from losing the other talents.

To summarize:

- Overall this season is good on <DevastationEvoker />
- <RisingFury /> has hit its peak of awkwardness in Raid.

## Devastation in M+

**TL;DR**

<Figure src="/static/images/dev-rant/dev-and-mplus.png" />

As has become tradition at this point <DevastationEvoker /> is just not built for M+ in one way or another.

<Scalecommander /> offers some pretty good damage and is absurdly efficient in outlier dungeons such as **King's Rest** (many small/medium sized pulls with near-equal priority targets), but is essentially the mortal enemy of anyone that likes funnel, is fundamentally unable to hit only one enemy and its AoE is rather bad for high target counts. As a result it at best makes it look better than it actually is and in reality it just is rather mediocre compared to the better classes and has established a reputation of "pad spec"/"anti-funnel" (derogative).

<Flameshaper /> is stuck in the dichotomy of "Good ST, Bad AoE" or "Bad ST, Good AoE" and as such is basically a different playstyle for lower output. It relies heavily on its cooldown windows which are hillariously strong to the point that few classes can compete with the output during <Dragonrage /> but the absolutely titanic duration of **55s** makes it exceedingly unwieldy. Additionally the rotation of it is rather single-minded and as such doesn't suit everyone.

As I already hinted at, <RisingFury /> in M+ is just horrible.

- The ramp caps out when half the mobs are dead so your output just looks worse than it actually is.
- The 20s lingering effect, previously called <RisenFury />, very often just ends up being wasted because packs rarely last long enough. This becomes worse the lower the key level is and at the same time ironically somehow doesn't get better on higher keys either.

All these factors just make <DevastationEvoker/> worse in M+ and as such you are constantly plagued by a feeling of mediocrity when playing it in M+.

## The Apex Problem

I simply cannot adequately express how bad <RisingFury /> feels. It already has gotten one major rework in Season 2 of Midnight which failed to address the problems stated above and at this point I am gonna propose 2 solutions:

### Option One: Rework It Again

The main two problems are the ramp and the lingering effect. Massively speed up the ramp to 5-10s at most and replace the lingering effect with more charges of <UnboundFlame />.
This would keep the overall identity of "getting stronger during the main cooldown and getting a lingering power boost after it" while addressing the main issues the current implementation has.

### Option Two: The Return of Obsidian Shards

There hasn't been a single season where I have not hoped for Obsidian Shards to make its way into the talents somehow. It is very non-intrusive while also reinforcing the rotation that most people are familiar with. However my preferred solution would have a few adjustments:

#### Unbound Flame

Simply speaking I'd rather not lose <UnboundFlame /> so I intend to move it to the talent tree. The exact position is unclear to me right now but there are plenty of talents I'd sacrifice for it. 

<Figure src="/static/images/dev-rant/uf-talents.png" />

The reasoning for these choices will become apparent in the talent section of this post which comes later but for now lets go over the replacement talents for the apex nodes

> Rank 1: <Disintegrate /> and <Pyre /> pierce enemies with <ObsidianShards />, dealing 18% of damage done as Volcanic damage over 8 sec. 

This is a carbon copy of the original <ObsidianShards /> effect. Nothing much to see here.
> Rank 2-3: Casting an empower or <UnboundFlame /> causes <ObsidianShards /> to blaze with power, increasing the damage of it by 100%/200% for 5s. This effect extends when reapplied.

Same as the first node, this is another copied mechanic, adjusted for the apex nodes. Additionally it has <UnboundFlame /> added for some more interaction with the kit and it now extends since it was a point of friction which would be rather annoying for the current iteration of <DevastationEvoker />.

> Rank 4: During <Dragonrage />, <ObsidianShards /> always blaze with power. Casting any of the spells that would amplify <ObsidianShards /> instead increase your haste by 15% for 5s. This effect extends when reapplied.

This node completes the array of effects that <ObsidianShards /> originally offered while also offering a new effect providing more haste during <Dragonrage />. 
Why? Because Haste is fun. 

Overall I think Option 2 is largely the better idea since <RisingFury /> is just forever tainted by the three seasons where its effect and comparable effects have ruined the spec.

## Hero Talents and Their Shortcomings

### Flameshaper

<Flameshaper /> is a pile of wasted potential with glimmers of genuinely great additions to <DevastationEvoker />. The core gameplay is solid and it generally is fun, but compared to its counterpart it lacks depth. It essentially plays like <DevastationEvoker abbreviated/> back in Dragonflight with minor nuances but these nuances don't dispell the general feeling of blandness.

Let's jump right into the list of problems:

#### Conduit of Flame

<ConduitOfFlame /> is a pretty simple talent and would be good if not for a single missing interaction. <ConduitOfFlame /> just *needs* to be affected by <Tyranny />. Right now, <ConduitOfFlame /> just brings back the problem of early Dragonflight where <DevastationEvoker abbreviated/> logged off in the low percentages of the boss fight and it is just *annoying*. It's understandable why reverse execute as a concept was explored, but it must also be recognized that this experiment was put to rest a while ago, through the myriad of attempts to reign in <DevastationEvoker abbreviated />s burst over the years. Leave it be.

#### Twin Flame

<TwinFlame /> is such a neat talent. It's a massive shame that its activation condition is so limited and that its general contribution to your output is so low. I'd love if it triggered on empower cast aswell to just increase the frequency of the flames you shape. This would also serve to increase its contribution and would help to make <Flameshaper/> more distinct from base <DevastationEvoker abbreviated/> and <Scalecommander/>.

#### Consume Flame
<ConsumeFlame /> is in a similar position as <ConduitOfFlame/>. I love the overall function but there is just one aspect that I loathe. The current function of <ConsumeFlame /> entirely eliminates any chance of <FireBreath/> ranks ever mattering. With the way it currently works, any rank but the lowest is just strictly inferior since you are actively wasting damage as the longer duration of the lower ranks is never a problem. So what is my solution to this conundrum? 

> <Disintegrate /> consumes 1.0 sec of <FireBreath /> from enemies it damages, detonating it for 150% of the amount consumed. <br />
> <Pyre /> consumes 4 sec of <FireBreath /> from enemies it damages, detonating it for 115% of the amount consumed. <br />
> Initial damage of <FireBreath /> is increased by 30%.

This change essentially serves two purposes:
1. Allow <FireBreath /> to be used at higher ranks if instant burst is needed while also reducing the amount of damage sacrificed.
2. Make higher ranks of <FireBreath /> the ideal solution in AoE target counts.

Obviously the number I buffed <FireBreath />'s instant damage by is mostly arbitrary but it needs to be significantly higher than the <Pyre/> bonus and lower than the <Disintegrate/> bonus or the idea won't work.

#### Draconic Instincts

<Flameshaper /> is significantly less sturdy to large hits than <Scalecommander />. Because of that I'd suggest two small changes to <DraconicInstincts />:
> Your wounds have a small chance to cauterize, transforming 20% of the damage taken into a heal absorb. Occurs more often from attacks that deal high damage, being guaraanteed for damage greater than 75% of your current health.

With these small changes, <Flameshaper /> now is a lot more sturdy and can live through the big hits which <Scalecommander /> could already survive thanks to <StretchTime /> and <HardenedScales />.

### Scalecommander

<Scalecommander /> is genuinely fine with a few small problems and as such is the polar opposite of <Flameshaper/>. The rotation is a lot stricter and more on rails but offers a nice flow. The main problems lie in what it absolutely cannot do: Priority Damage. With that lets head into the list:

#### Onslaught

There is no nicer way to say this: <Onslaught /> is not worth the the investment. It provides a single <Burnout /> proc per fight which is just such a measly increase that it won't even register on any reasonable scale. I have no solution to improve this talent other than replacing it entirely. It's bad bad. <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> also doesn't care about it and that spec hecking looooves to cast <LivingFlame /> due to a lack of better options.

#### Mass Disintegrate
Essentially what I intend to do with <MassDisintegrate /> is rebalance its damage distribution. I believe that it should always deal more damage to the primary target since there are so often targets which are either not relevant right now or entirely immune. The idea goes as follows:

> Empower spells cause your next <Disintegrate /> to strike up to 3 targets. The primary beam does 20% more damage for each possible additional target and secondary beams do 80% damage.

This wording is pretty vague so lets provide an example. Lets assume your maximum target count with <MassDisintegrate /> is 4 as it usually is right now then the following would occur:

| targets | primary damage | secondary damage |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| 1 Target | 160% | 0x80% |
| 2 Target | 160% | 1x80% |
| 3 Target | 160% | 2x80% |
| 4 Target | 160% | 3x80% |

This way you can always focus the <Disintegrate /> beam on the target you want to prioritize and don't need to despair when the secondary beams decide to hit the add thats immune and as such reducing your output.

## Spec Talents

Because of my involvement in theorycrafting, I have a certain expertise in the matter of talent selection and as such intend to use this fact to lend the following statements credibility.
Most of <DevastationEvoker />'s talents are fine at what they do but theres some decidely bad talents for one or more of these reasons:
- They are low value
- They have no use case
- They actively make the gameplay worse

Most of the proposed changes generally follow these rules:
- It makes the talent worth using
- It makes the talent interesting
- It fixes the problem the talent causes in the gameplay

### Low Value
#### Azure / Ruby Essence Burst
<EssenceBurst /> lies at the core of all Evoker specs but for some reason <DevastationEvoker /> needs two talents where the other specs have one. There is no reason other than padding out the tree and as such I'd prefer to merge both talents to make room for another low-impact core talent.

#### Ruby Embers
<RubyEmbers /> is just simply too weak. It buffs the weakest spell in the kit by roughly 10% and generally has no other impact. The position of Ruby Embers is a prime candidate for <UnboundFlame />.

#### Azure Sweep
<AzureSweep /> is a very solid addition to the rotation but it simply cannot compete with its counterpart of 8% damage to <Disintegrate /> and <AzureStrike />, regardless of hero tree. In an effort to cement my belief that empowers should be upranked, I propose to add a 10% damage buff to <AzureSweep /> when hitting targets that were hit by <EternitySurge /> in the last 10s.

#### Lay Waste
<LayWaste /> is in this list for a single reason: <Flameshaper />. <DeepBreath /> is simply too weak for <Flameshaper /> considering it has a **2 minute cooldown**. It should deal roughly 50% more damage or interact with the kit in another way because it simply cannot compete with <Pyre /> with any amount of talent investment.

#### Font of Magic
I cannot talk about one of these without mentioning the other so I've grouped them. <FontOfMagic /> is just generally a boring talent as the added rank for both empowers generally doesn't change anything about either gameplay or output. Additionally it makes no sense as a precursor to <ScorchingEmbers /> for reasons stated below. <FontOfMagic /> realistically should increase the damage dealt by empower casts.

### Rarely Used
#### Power Nexus
Technically <PowerNexus /> qualifies for both rarely used and bad value and that alone is a testament to how bad it is. <PowerNexus /> is immensely insignificant but man, does it feel nice to play with 6 Essences. This is my personal pick for the low-impact core talent that replaces one of the <EssenceBurst /> talents.

#### Feed the Flames / Engulfing Blaze
I grouped both of these together because <EngulfingBlaze /> is usually taken with <FeedTheFlames />. <EngulfingBlaze /> does its job as a precursor to <FeedTheFlames /> well but is also too detached from the function of <FeedTheFlames />. <FeedTheFlames /> is very strong but its too niche, which is a massive shame as the Firestorm visual is one of the best effects in the game. In an effort to increase both the cohesion between the talents and the versatility of <FeedTheFlames />, I propose this:
> After casting 6 <Pyre />s or <LivingFlame />, your next <Pyre /> or <LivingFlame /> will explode into a <Firestorm />.

To clarify on a niche interaction: <LivingFlame />'s from <LeapingFlames /> should count individually.

### Worse Gameplay
#### Scorching Embers
<ScorchingEmbers /> is one of the talents that wants you to never uprank <FireBreath />. This is easily resolved by decoupling the damage amp effect from the duration of <FireBreath />. The other option is to replace it with a better red-focused capstone talent but I cannot think of something thats remotely as universal.

## Various Issues and Requests
This is the last section before I am done complaining about things I care about. This section contains all the issues and requests that don't really fit into any category but just need to be mentioned for one reason or another:

#### Issues
- <ConsumeFlame /> does not reattribute <AugmentationEvoker /> buffs at all, making raid logs basically fully inaccurate and thus absurdly hard to compare the moment an <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> is present. 
- <Unravel /> occasionally only hits one target instead of all targets hit by <FireBreath />.
- All empowers can fail to cast and lock the cast until you cast something else.
- All empowers can fail to cast putting the spell on cooldown.

#### Requests
- <DeepBreath /> should be steerable by default to equalize the flight time and as such the damage output and because it generally feels better.
- <RenewingBlaze /> makes <ObsidianScales /> too strong of a cooldown and as such just leads to wasted value. Usable <RenewingBlaze /> was a far better option at a insignificant amount of added complexity.
- <Zephyr /> is just massively unintuitive and needs someone to maintain and update a list every single season to provide a reasonable amount of understanding. Consider changing the effect away from Avoidance.
- <TimeStop /> is a super neat spell that is unfortunately locked to PvP. I'd love to have it for PvE aswell.
- <BestowWeyrnstone /> is a very niche piece of utility and should not be locked to <AugmentationEvoker />.
