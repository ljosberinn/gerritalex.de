## Midnight Season 1 Changes Fallout

It's no secret that <Scalecommander /> had a strong showing in all 12.0 content. <Bombardments />-powered cooldown reduction fed into <Duplicate /> which ended up being a significant portion of its damage through duplicate Duplicates. 12.0 also had a strong tier set for <AugmentationEvoker /> which further reinforced said feedback loop.

Too much, as it turned out, as that loop got canned in a very unsatisfying manner going into Midnight Season 2. The hero tree now feels like a shell of a former self, but at least it is still distinct to <Chronowarden />. Some of that was deserved as the specific interaction of "<Bombardments /> Pooling and Spreading" was not universally liked and left <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> as one of the most complex specializations in Midnight, correcting a likely oversight. At the same time, there were a hundred less sledgehammer-sized approaches one could have taken without getting ridiculed for calling <Bombardments />-spreading "unintuitive gameplay" - which effectively was tab-targeting.

Meanwhile, <Chronowarden /> saw very few changes. It already had its raid niche, but frustrating talents such as <DoubleTime /> or <Temporality />, not to mention entirely dead talents such as <TimeConvergence /> and <MotesOfAcceleration /> saw at best consistency fixes rather than tuning.

## It Is Not All Doom and Gloom

## Where Are We Now?

### Positional Requirements Suck

<Prescience /> used to be the targeting mechanism for <EbonMight />. Now with <EbonMight /> being
raid-wide, you can cast <Prescience /> targeting the boss or actively choose your targets using unit
frames.

The former yields good enough results to be viable which is nice, definitely not something that could've been said last expansion. However, one targeting mechanism was not updated: that of <ShiftingSands />.

Casting an Empower grants **your closest ally** (following generic buff rules preferring DPS specs) that isn't already affected by your <ShiftingSands /> its effect. It does not care, unlike <Prescience />, whether the target has cooldowns active.

Such a solution is _somewhat_ fine for a raid setting in which everyone plays at roughly the same skill level and naturally scales well into competitive realms. It is however terrible for anything below that:

- less coordinated groups commonly have a larger skill gap between individual players. This makes it tremendously difficult to ensure having consistent throughput per pull.
- less skilled/consistent players don't tend to position themselves at the same time in the same spot, making <AugmentationEvoker /> the only spec that has a considerable amount of its throughput depending on things fully out of their control
- don't even get me started on how terrible this is for pugging...

##### ...and how to solve it

The solution is rather simple: have <ShiftingSands /> follow <Prescience /> targeting. You'd still have to tie on distance between all currently affected <Prescience />-targets. It automatically addresses all problems:

- positional requirement vanishes nearly completely
- throughput optimization is a lot more predictable
- as <Prescience /> prefers targets in cooldowns, so will thus also <ShiftingSands /> which is what you want anyway
  - for autotargeting players this is also a potentially significant buff and quality-of-life improvement, as ironically they often feel pressured into playing around this despite needing it the least

Proposed logic:

```
> no Prescience active? --> closest ally
>> no cooldowns active? --> closest Prescience target
>>> multiple cooldowns active? --> longest active remaining duration.
                                   if that one already has your Shifting Sands, proceed to the next
```

### Throughput Variance Sucks

In my mind, from a tuning perspective it makes little sense to have a spec have wildly different throughput depending on factors outside of the player's control. <ShiftingSands /> as mentioned is such a cause, but <DoubleTime /> and <MotesOfPossibility /> are others.

The swings you can see - especially during progress when positioning is not necessarily solved yet - between these scenarios are absurd:

> oh my <ShiftingSands /> landed on the right people

> oh, my motes procced <ShiftingSands /> on a good target

> oh, my motes procced fucking <SymbioticBloom /> on my best dps who ran away from me a splitsecond before my Empower finished

> oh I guess I just crit my <EbonMight /> via <DoubleTime /> **three** times in a row due to <BreathOfEons /> -> <EbonMight /> -> <TimeSkip /> -> <EbonMight />

And to make it worse, these compound so you can have absolute absurdly good pulls or below average and there's nothing you can do about it.

Oh and fun fact: you cannot tell whether <Prescience /> crit via <DoubleTime /> or not. Only the tooltip holds the info, so it's invisible in actual gameplay.

##### ...and how to solve it

Again, potentially controversial but we're here to stir the pot a bit after all.

1. Remove or rework <MotesOfPossibility />. I do not think it is salvageable as-is. Too much of it is RNG. On top of that, their movement behaviour still trying to buff the origin <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> (by floating towards them) is often frustrating at best as all possible buffs except for <InfernosBlessing /> are lackluster on ourselves.
   - remember <WowheadLink kind="item" id={204177} icon="inv_hand_1h_evokerlegendary_d_01">Nasz'uro, the Unbound Legacy</WowheadLink>? maybe don't have it activate on empowers because so much depends on them already but could repurpose that buff.
2. Change <DoubleTime />:

> <EbonMight /> and <Prescience /> effects are permanently increased by a fraction of your current
> critical strike chance.

This aims to achieve three goals:

- remove rng, add reliability
- still follow the theme of "do something with crit on <Chronowarden />"
- still follow the theme of <Chronowarden /> by being buff-focused
- still fully passive

In practice, I'd imagine a factor on this:

- <EbonMight />s effect gets increased by the current crit chance at 50% rate, i.e. 30% crit means
  15% increased Ebon Might value
- <Prescience />s effect gets increased by 2x the crit chance _at cast time_, i.e. 30% crit means
  <Prescience /> goes from 3% base effect to `3*(1+2*0.3) = 4.8%`

### Triple Punishment For Player Deaths in Dungeons

On key level 12 and above, you already get punished twice for a death:

- the player death costs you time because of the dps loss and resurrection management can be complicated, e.g. if your sole source of a combat rez ability died and you now have to use engineering rez, or simply because you don't have a rez available
- it costs you 15 seconds because of the affix

However, when a DPS dies other than the <AugmentationEvoker />, you also lose ~50% of the buff damage <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> provides because <EbonMight /> does not adjust its scaling below 2 buff targets. Even when the DPS is quickly resurrected, you cannot necessarily reapply <EbonMight /> instantly depending when you casted it.

Why? No other spec, especially no other _DPS_ spec, has this penalty. And even _with_ <EbonMight /> rescaling which is only reasonable, you still lost one of your <ShiftingSands /> and <Prescience /> and thus <FateMirror /> targets so even then, there's still some loss.

The solution is simple: have <EbonMight /> autoadjust even below 2 targets.

### Survivability Issues in High Keys

...or should I say evokers in general? It's a long standing fact and even meme that especially new evokers tend to flop over in all content. This has two reasons:

- especially <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> is inherently not very tanky
- except for <DefyFate /> which only counts for comedic effect, all evoker defensives are proactive

At the same time, <AugmentationEvoker /> repeatedly had its survivability nerfed across various patches:

- Most notably, <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> is the only evoker spec that still gains only 4% magic damage reduction from <InherentResistance /> while all others gain 8%.
- <DraconicLegacy /> increases stamina only by 6% for <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> and <PreservationEvoker abbreviated /> & <DevastationEvoker abbreviated /> gain the full 8%.

##### ...and how to solve it

Some ideas were brought up before already: talent adjustments to <AspectsFavor /> and <MoltenBlood />. The latter wouldn't be very relevant for raid since it's yet another proactive defensive. And as much as I'd like to see changes to aforementioned dead talents, removing the penalties on <InherentResistance /> and <DraconicLegacy /> would already help.

### Half-Baked Set Design in 12.1

It is no secret that every patch, some specs win, some specs lose. I actually do not know how strong <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated plural /> set is currently and I personally don't mind its rare focus on <Prescience />. Last time we had that was 10.2, Amirdrassil, so it's been 3 years.

What however has me at a loss is the following interaction from our <Spell id={1296638} icon={false}>current 4-set bonus</Spell>:

- casting <Upheaval /> grants <MagnifiedFate />

That's the state of 12.1. Sounds good on paper, but...

- it did not work with our <Timewalker />
- it does not extend on <Upheaval /> recasts after <TimeSkip />, _the_ main ability of <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> in raid in 12.0 and 12.1
- it does not even pandemic!

With 12.1.5, at least the first point was addressed, 9 weeks later. This still leads to wasteful gameplay:

- the <Accretion /> talent reduces cooldown of <Upheaval /> per <Eruption /> cast
- after <TimeSkip />, since you're in your big burst window, you want to cast your empowers asap so putting <Upheaval /> on cooldown is very much wanted
- ...especially because the <Spell id={1296637} icon={false}>current 2-set bonus</Spell> reduces <Upheaval /> cooldown!
- ...but your <Spell id={1296638} icon={false}>current 4-set bonus</Spell> prevents you from doing that
- ...yet you still want to cast _something_ and due to the resource overflow <Chronowarden /> has in these windows, you're forced to waste <Accretion /> value by casting <Eruption /> because you're sitting at max essences and likely some <EssenceBurst /> which you want to spend because of <MomentumShift />

> **Just recast <Upheaval />** ...

...you might say. Sure! Then I'll be throwing away the 4-set bonus. Which certainly can't be the design intent.

> **Just untalent <Accretion />**...

With the 2-set bonus reducing <Upheaval /> cooldown which also powers <ShiftingSands /> and <Accretion /> gating <RumblingEarth /> this is obviously not viable.

Very simple fix of making it fully extend and pandemic, yet here we are. Please think these more through.

### Feast & Famine

Let's talk essence economy.

<DevastationEvoker /> is an established long-time complainer about this problem. That spec plays nicely, but only because of <ImminentDestruction variant="devastation" /> and <PowerSwell />. Missing especially the latter feels painful and you can end up in situations spamming <LivingFlame /> hoping for an <EssenceBurst /> proc. <PowerSwell /> has its own problem of overwriting its buff when you send both empowers after each other, conflicting inherently with <Causality />, forcing you to spread your empowers, but this is an <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> post after all so we'll keep that rant for another time.

<AugmentationEvoker /> has a similar problem, but experience varies heavily depending on the hero tree:
- <Chronowarden /> has an essence overflow whenever you press <TipTheScales /> due to <TemporalBurst />
  - this effect is further amplified whenever combined with <TimeSkip /> and thus even more with <TomorrowToday />
  - since <TemporalBurst /> affects <TipTheScales /> (more about that [directly below](#cancelauraing-is-optimal-for-chronowarden)) and <TemporalBurst /> as of 12.1.5 is also affected by <Timewalker />, it's active a considerable amount of time
- <Scalecommander /> can feel quite similar to <DevastationEvoker abbreviated />, except <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> has no <PowerSwell /> equivalent
  - you have less Haste inherently due to a lack of <TemporalBurst />, as Haste increases essence recovery speed
  - you could of course stack more Haste as we did in Season 1, but even at peak Haste of that season, you could end up in the Valley of Despair (press 8 <LivingFlame />, do not collect <EssenceBurst />, straight to jail)
  - currently, stacking more Haste is not very viable in this season since Mastery is so strong

### Spec Talents and Lack of Diversity

If you were to look at the <AugmentationEvoker /> spec tree across all content you might get the impression it's actually fairly diverse and offers meaningful choices:

- <span className="text-green-500">Green talents are always played in some content.</span>

    Yellow talents see situational play, either in raid depending on the encounter or because they

  </span>
- <span className="text-red-500">Red talents are never played at all.</span>

You now might think "great, that looks healthy, only 4 red nodes", but the community has grown to dislike the status quo for the following reasons.

#### Staleness

We play the same talents now since the introduction of hero trees, around 2.25 years currently. The only differences since then were down to tuning between <Scalecommander /> and <Chronowarden />, AoE vs ST and other talents that have since then left the premises again - you will not be missed <Rockfall /> and <MoltenEmbers /> o7.

It's so stale, the red talents we never pick have also not been picked in years, if ever:

<FontOfMagic /> mirrors a talent all evoker specs have. Why is it not in the class tree if all have access to it anyway? Not that we'd pick it but at least frees some space up for a hopefully more interesting alternative.

<SymbioticBloom /> is straight up terrible on all ends:

- <EmeraldBlossom /> does painfully little healing unless you have a <NourishingSands /> proc which is exceedingly unlikely in group content due to needing to last hit enemies which is extremely unlikely as <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated />
- for <SymbioticBloom /> to activate, allies need to be close to also get affected
- <SymbioticBloom /> does not reattribute in logs, so its impact is painful to work out - once you do actually go there you'll unfortunately find that its impact is irrelevant, in the low double digits of hps at best - it costing 2 points is also just not reasonable

The <ProlongLife /> / <DreamOfSpring /> choice node saw meme play in the DF S3 MDI for the start of Dawn of the Infinite: Murozond's Rise - never before, never after, as they're painfully costly and absurdly tuned.

<AspectsFavor /> was once a ~~good~~ very strong talent and a must-pick for high keys, but got repeatedly nerfed and was never considered again.

#### Some Talents Are Left To Rot

<AspectsFavor /> only saw nerfs, and as of writing, hasn't seen tuning in over 2 years, despite being a fairly central node in the tree.

<PerilousFate /> never saw any tuning at all since it competes with the always-superior <ChronoWard />.

<MoltenBlood /> saw repeated nerfs, then nothing for a long time and only cosmetic buffs since then.

#### ...and how to solve it

<FontOfMagic />

Move it into the class tree and/or make it actually matter for <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> to uprank.

<SymbioticBloom />

Make it baseline for <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated />s version of <EmeraldBlossom />. <EmeraldBlossom abbreviated /> is already basically never pressed, might aswell.

Absolute minimum: reduce it to a single-point talent. This would still be a cosmetic change of goodwill because you will still not see anyone play it.

<ProlongLife /> / <DreamOfSpring />

Remove them and come up with interesting alternatives that compete with adjacent talents such as <FateMirror /> and <Anachronism /> that aren't massive DPS losses.

I'll be blunt here: these talents feed into a spec fantasy - <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> as supporting healer - that never was adopted nor is viable in practice. Whenever you'd need better healing, you'd just add another healer in raid. Whenever you'd need better healing in keys, you either reroll the healer to a better spec, plan your defensives better, do a different pull, etc. - but you don't drop significant DPS for a vague HPS gain that may or may not be enough because it's stupidly hard to quantify.

<AspectsFavor />

There's a unique chance here to address [some later feedback](#survivability-issues-in-high-keys) when considering options for this talent. I'm thinking of a more player-focused approach rather than leaning into the now-gutted party-wide utility it originaly held. As a one pointer, it could become:

```
Black Attunements is twice as effective on yourself.

Bronze Attunements grants 5% Haste to yourself.
```

This is also still very much in line with the talent name itself.

Absolute minimum: reduce it to a single-point talent. Same as <SymbioticBloom /> - this would still be a cosmetic change of goodwill because you will still not see anyone play it.

<PerilousFate />

It's not even that <ChronoWard /> is still so busted after its repeated nerfs, it's just that its effect never matters. Tanks don't _really_ care about attack speed debuffs, <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> like any other DPS is not a spec that loves holding their major offensive cooldowns for anything really and especially not for when the tank needs it.

In my opinion it's best to remove this talent entirely.

<MoltenBlood />

Many players have become enjoyers of <RegenerativeChitin /> and reasonably so, it's just really nice QoL that is not expensive to get. <MoltenBlood /> also had problematic use-cases in the past, being abused as external for other specs which - assuming here - by modern design decisions would not be an intended use. That of course could be solved by disallowing casting it on roles other than tanks.

Absolute minimum: don't bind the scaling to spell power, but instead to max health of the target so it never gets forgotten again _and_ disallow casting it on non-tank roles, other than yourself:

```
When cast, Blistering Scales grants the target a shield that absorbs damage equal to a percentage of their maximum health [5-20%], based on their missing health. Lower health targets gain a larger shield.

Can only be cast on yourself, or tank specializations.
```

Note that this also ties in with solving some later mentioned survivability issues. In combination they'd likely be too strong, however I do particularily liking the idea of situationally giving up some damage in favor of surviving by having to cast <BlisteringScales /> on yourself.

### Cancelauraing Is Optimal For Chronowarden

Pressing <TipTheScales /> triggers <TemporalBurst /> for <Chronowarden />.

<TemporalBurst /> starts the moment you cast it, not when you consume the aura by casting an empower. <TipTheScales /> only goes on cooldown once you consume it.

Thus, whenever you cannot combine <TipTheScales /> with <TimeSkip />, either because you simply don't have <TimeSkip /> or because it's on cooldown, it is optimal to instantly `/cancelaura Tip The Scales` (on a different keybind, e.g. <TimeSkip />!) to maximize the cooldown reduction on <TipTheScales />.

This frankly quite stupid optimization has been a thing ever since we were graced with hero trees. It's been reported repeatedly. New players continuously trip over it. I refuse to believe this is intended. I also refuse to believe that literally nobody looked into addressing it. So why are we in this state still?

The fix could be simple: <TemporalBurst /> is only granted once <TipTheScales /> is consumed.

### Zephyr and Intuitiveness

Every now and then we get largely much appreciated Developer's notes in tuning patch notes. Sometimes these only make you shake your head. Here's one of those, from [11.2 (Manaforge Omega tier)](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/24226698/ghosts-of-k-aresh-content-update-notes):

> The following talents have been removed:
>
> (...)
>
> Spell Block
>
> Developers' notes: Spell Block **required a lot of specialized knowledge to use. Not only did you need to know when damage was coming, but also whether it was a form of magic damage that could be blocked or not and thus affected by the ability.** AOE damage isn't blockable, neither is periodic damage. Some tankbusters are and others aren't. We weren't happy with the knowledge demanded to use the ability effectively.

Yet somehow, <Zephyr /> exists. Unfortunately it needs a **[lot](/blog/tww-s2-zephyr) [of](/blog/tww-s3-zephyr) [specialized](/blog/midnight-s1-aoe) [knowledge](/blog/midnight-s2-aoe) to use** and this also applies to <NimbleFlyer />. It is far from intuitive what is and what isn't AoE, in fact it is pretty damn inconsistent.

Also, why is the <Zephyr /> radius only 20y, lower than our cast range? <TimeSpiral /> is somehow 40y.

### Bugs?

In contrast to some other specs, luckily <AugmentationEvoker /> is not riddled with significant bugs. The most impactful ones are shared with all evoker specs, such as:

- recharging essences are often incorrect
- empowers randomly don't go off

Both of these are present ever since the evoker class was added, you'll find bug reports going back to [November 2022](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/evoker-essence-not-displaying-correctly/1407169), from the Dragonflight Beta.

Returning to <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> specific problems.

#### Sense Power Tracking is Incomplete

<Prescience />, regardless of whether we enable <SensePower />, prefers allies using "powerful
abilities". That list is datamineable and also naturally testable in-game through tedious
observation.

The following spells are NOT triggers and thus missing:

- <UnholyDeathKnight />: <ArmyOfTheDead />
- <FrostDeathKnight />: <BreathOfSindragosa />
- <ElementalShaman />: <Ascendance />

#### Incomplete Cooldown Manager

Generally being blessed with a largely complete cooldown manager, some abilities yet remain to be seen even after so much time. This is particularily frustrating, as it is no longer possible for addons to extend the cooldown manager without extremely tainting it, ending up in an unplayable state.

I don't want a cooldown manager addon just to patch up a clear lack of maintenance when implementing a specs abilities. These were literally 10 second fixes for any even remotely competent WeakAuras user before 12.0, but for some reason we need to report these over and over again and still these don't get addressed or even publicly acknowledged.

_Multiple_ devs also told me personally at BlizzCon that these are easy to hotfix and they're happy to do so, they just need to be made aware. Which has me again wondering about the organisational/management issue that seems to be going on behind the scenes.

- <Temporality /> does not show its current damage reduction as stacks. This is an aura issue - the
  damage reduction is solely communicated through cooldown text which is inaccessible to addons. The
  aura should indicate the current damage reduction as stacks, then it should work automatically.
- <Burnout /> and <AncientFlame /> are missing for <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated />, but are
  present for <DevastationEvoker abbreviated />
- <NourishingSands /> and <MotesOfAcceleration /> are missing
- if you start a key or login while <TipTheScales /> is already active, the cooldown manager fails to recognize that and doesn't show its "pressed" state
- <EbonMight /> dynamically pandemics based on <Timewalker />. The cooldown manager statically uses
  the base value, so its completely misleading
- <SleepWalk />, <Landslide /> and <OppressingRoar /> auras are only tracked for your target, not
  just any affected, making it useless
  - the first two are at least tracked on nameplates

#### Motes and Double-time

<MotesOfPossibility /> can proc <Prescience /> on whoever picks them up, but while talented into
<DoubleTime /> they should also be able to crit. They cannot.

### Log Attribution

Naturally a pet peeve of mine, but it's now been ~3.5 years since the spec got introduced and we're still battling [a long list of abilities](https://gist.github.com/ljosberinn/a2f08a53cfe8632a18350eea44e9da3e) that do not communicate how much of their damage was because of the <WowheadSpecIcon spec="augmentation-evoker">Aug</WowheadSpecIcon> buffing the damaging player. No shade on the poor souls having to figure out some of these, particularily accumulators but please?

It's currently completely impossible to tell what your actual throughput is if you play with any of these specs:

- <WowheadSpecIcon spec="demo-warlock">Demonology Warlock</WowheadSpecIcon>
- <WowheadSpecIcon spec="bm-hunter">Beast Mastery Hunter</WowheadSpecIcon>

And it's decently misleading but at least not unreadable if you play with any of these:

- <WowheadSpecIcon spec="havoc-dh">Havoc Demon Hunter</WowheadSpecIcon>
- <WowheadSpecIcon spec="sub-rogue">Subtlety Rogue</WowheadSpecIcon>
- <WowheadSpecIcon spec="assa-rogue">Assasination Rogue</WowheadSpecIcon>
- <WowheadSpecIcon spec="elemental-shaman">Elemental Shaman</WowheadSpecIcon>

Nothing we can do here except wait and see what 12.2.5 holds or whenever the in-game damage meter natively receives support for this...

##### ...and how to solve it

This is going to be controversial and it would naturally take away some of the class fantasy, but my naive idea here is to "simply" have <AugmentationEvoker abbreviated /> only provide flat % damage increase as well as raw damage hits/procs such as e.g. <Bombardments /> or <FateMirror />. It also fixes synergy issues with other specs that (don't) scale well with mainstat for example, or specs that have no real cooldowns (<WowheadSpecIcon spec="bm-hunter">Beast Mastery Hunter</WowheadSpecIcon>, <WowheadSpecIcon spec="outlaw-rogue">Outlaw Rogue</WowheadSpecIcon>, <WowheadSpecIcon spec="fury-warrior">Fury Warrior</WowheadSpecIcon>). Clearly this entire reattribution thing hasn't been working out.
